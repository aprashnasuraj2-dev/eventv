    Race week, race and ticket dates

    The Automobile Club de l’Ouest’s official event and ticket pages describe the 2027 edition as 9–13 June. The FIA World Endurance Championship race page displays 6–13 June. A broader WEC calendar range and an organiser’s event or ticket range may cover different activities; without a source explaining the exact difference, do not assume the earlier range means general public admission begins on 6 June.

    The official ticket site describes distinct products, including a race-weekend ticket valid Saturday and Sunday. A full-week product, grandstand, camping or parking option can have different access and conditions. Check the actual product’s date, area, named holder, fees, transfer and refund terms. A sale opening on a past date does not prove a particular ticket remains in stock.

    The 24-hour competition, supporting events, practice and spectator admission are related but different schedules. A race-week date does not establish a start clock, and the end of a 24-hour arithmetic interval need not match the official final classification. Recheck the organiser’s detailed 2027 programme when available.

    How the rotation works

    For a hypothetical 15:00 start, two people and six-hour shifts, person 1 covers 15:00–21:00, person 2 covers 21:00–03:00 the next day, and the pattern repeats until 15:00 the next day. Those times are invented to show the calculation. The tool labels a next-day clock explicitly so that 03:00 is not mistaken for an earlier time on the starting day.

    When the shift length does not divide 24, the last shift is shorter. For example, five-hour shifts produce four five-hour blocks and one four-hour block. With three people, a simple rotation assigns blocks cyclically; the amount assigned to each person may differ. Discuss the plan with the group instead of assuming equal totals or adequate rest.

    The planner does not account for breaks, changes of location, handover time, sleep needs, medical needs or a race interruption. It is a viewing worksheet, not an instruction to stay awake, drive while tired or leave a child unattended. Use the official event programme and make a practical rest and return plan.

    Access, camping and the journey home

    1. Check official admission dates, the named ticket product and whether a grandstand, campsite or parking reservation is separate.
    2. Verify the actual race and track schedule, circuit entrances, accessible viewing and permitted items in the current organiser guidance.
    3. Check transport services, pedestrian routes and any road restrictions for both arrival and departure. A 24-hour model cannot tell you when a particular train or shuttle runs.
    4. Plan weather protection, food, water, rest and a meeting point. Ask the organiser about individual accessibility or companion requirements.
    5. For international travel, consult the responsible French and Schengen authorities for the passport and entry rules that apply to you.
