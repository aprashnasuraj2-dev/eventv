Know the date boundaries

The official calendar lists the festival from 10 to 21 February, the European Film Market from 10 to 16 February, the Berlinale Co-Production Market from 13 to 16 February, and Berlinale Talents from 12 to 17 February. All four named date ranges overlap on 13–16 February, but that overlap does not imply a single badge admits the holder to every activity.

The organiser says the official awards ceremony is on Friday 19 February, followed by the first two Audience Days on 20 and 21 February. Those dates are useful for planning; they do not identify award winners, the films available on each audience day, or ticket availability.

For example, the lookup reports the festival, European Film Market, Co-Production Market and Berlinale Talents on 16 February. On 17 February, the latter two market ranges have ended while the festival and Talents range remain. The result is a simple inclusive calendar comparison, not an individual programme.

Accreditation is not the same as a screening ticket

Berlinale describes distinct routes for professionals, market participants, students, press and younger audiences. It also says full participation in the Co-Production Market and as a Berlinale Talent has separate application procedures. Review the current requirements for your own role rather than assuming a festival accreditation covers all programmes.

The organiser says specified Festival, Student and Press Accreditation holders need an advance online ticket for festival screenings and public Berlinale Talents talks. A badge and a booked session ticket are different things. The exact booking window, access scope, eligibility and capacity depend on the current official instructions and the product.

For public visitors, consult the festival’s public ticket information when the 2027 sale details and programme are available. A historical price or 2026 booking rule is not proof of a 2027 offer. This guide does not claim live availability or a specific ticket price.

Build a realistic Berlin itinerary

If a screening ends at 14:00, the next starts at 15:15, and you allow 45 minutes for transfer and entry, the tool reports a 30-minute margin. That result assumes both events occur at the entered times, that you have valid tickets and that the allowance is sufficient. A late-running Q&A, queue or transit disruption can erase the margin.

Check the address and entrance for each venue, current Berlin transport schedules, step-free access and local conditions. A film festival spans multiple venues and a map’s straight-line distance is not a reliable door-to-door time. If you have a disability or need a companion arrangement, confirm the specific venue and screening directly with the organiser.

  1. Check the latest official programme and verify whether the event is public, accredited or invitation-only.
  2. Confirm the specific badge, separate screening ticket, booking deadline and refund conditions.
  3. Leave time for transfer, security and entry; avoid double-counting Q&A if it is already included in the listed end.
  4. For international travel, consult responsible German and Schengen authorities about your own entry requirements.
