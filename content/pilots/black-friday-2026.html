Evidence state · checked November 6, 2026

The date is easy. Proving the discount is the hard part.

Consumer-protection rules differ materially by market. This hub separates a retailer’s marketing claim from your own calculation and labels each country rule. It is a practical verifier, not legal advice and not a substitute for the seller’s exact contract or local law.

DATE RULE

Nov 27, 2026

OPM lists U.S. Thanksgiving on Nov 26, 2026. This hub derives Black Friday as the following Friday; OPM does not designate Black Friday as a federal holiday.

US

Fictitious former price ≠ bargain

Current eCFR Part 233 says an artificial inflated former price can make the advertised bargain false. FTC shopping guidance also says to read deal terms, condition, shipping and refund policies.

DE / EU

30-day reference rule

For announced reductions, EU rules require the reference to identify the lowest price applied in at least the previous 30 days. That makes a dated history especially useful for Germany.

CA

Ordinary selling price must be genuine

Canada’s Competition Bureau says a made-up regular price can be illegal; it describes volume and time tests for substantiating a regular-price claim.

AU

“Was/now” and total price must not mislead

ACCC guidance warns against sale claims based on a price that was not genuinely used and against headline prices that omit unavoidable parts of the payable amount.

UK

Total price + distance returns matter

CMA guidance says total prices should be clear and complete. GOV.UK says many online buyers can notify cancellation within 14 days of receipt and then have another 14 days to return, subject to exceptions.

Historical enforcement signal, not a 2026 forecast: a European Commission sweep covering 314 online traders during the 2025 Black Friday/Cyber Monday period reported that at least 30% referenced discounts incorrectly. The lesson is to retain dated price evidence, not to assume a particular 2026 retailer is non-compliant.

Mechanism first

Three prices can describe three different realities.

AAdvertised reference

MSRP, RRP, “was”, list or regular price shown by the seller. Useful only if its meaning and authenticity are understood.

BVerified baseline

A price you can evidence: recent genuine selling price, 30-day low where relevant, your own dated snapshot, or another defensible comparison.

CLanded cost

What leaves your wallet after coupon/rebate, mandatory fees, delivery and estimated tax/duty. Cheapest headline price can lose here.

System counterexample: a product can have the largest headline percentage discount and still be the worse purchase if its verified baseline was already low, its seller is third-party, delivery is expensive, the return window is poor, or the “same” model is refurbished. Component discount ≠ total purchase quality.

Refurbished / used evidence

The condition label changes the comparison set.

FTC

U.S. consumer guidance warns that terms such as “refurbished,” “vintage” and “close-out” can indicate less-than-perfect condition; marketplace shoppers should inspect seller identity, item photos, fees and return policies.

Canada

Federal guidance says online listings should identify whether an item is new, refurbished or used and recommends saving product information; Health Canada advises checking repair/refurbishment history and recalls.

Australia

ACCC guidance says second-hand/refurbished status, age, price and condition affect what quality/durability can reasonably be expected.

UK / EU

Consumer rights can still apply to commercially sold used goods, but condition, seller type and specific rights differ. Private-seller transactions are materially different from professional-trader sales.

Failure modes

Eight ways a “deal” comparison breaks.

  1. Different SKU: storage, generation, panel, CPU, bundle or model suffix changed.
  2. Different seller: marketplace third party is treated as if it were the platform/retailer.
  3. Different condition: refurbished/open-box price is compared with a new-item reference.
  4. Fake baseline: MSRP/RRP is assumed to be the recent real selling price.
  5. Coupon asymmetry: member-only, trade-in, financing or targeted coupon is treated as available to everyone.
  6. Landed-cost omission: delivery, mandatory fees, tax/duty or required add-ons are ignored.
  7. Return asymmetry: cheaper seller has materially worse return shipping/restocking or no voluntary change-of-mind policy.
  8. Timing bias: one Black Friday screenshot is called “lowest price” without a dated history.

No live retailer price, inventory, coupon, tax, delivery, return-policy, warranty, condition, Black Friday or Cyber Monday feed is active. All calculators operate only on values you enter; official-source summaries are a dated editorial snapshot.
