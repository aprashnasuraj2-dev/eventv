Fast enough to qualify. But was it fast enough to clear the cutoff?

The Boston Marathon is scheduled for Monday, April 19, 2027. The B.A.A. confirmed a 5-minute 17-second competitive acceptance cutoff for its 2027 open-field qualifier process. A qualifying standard, the competitive cutoff and an individual acceptance notification are three different things.

Qualification & acceptance threshold checker

Enter the age you will be on April 19, 2027, not your age on qualifying race day. Open-field standards only; adaptive and wheelchair divisions use different rules.

For net downhill: below 1,500 ft no adjustment; 1,500–2,999 ft add 5:00; 3,000–5,999 ft add 10:00; 6,000+ ft ineligible. An official course determination takes precedence.

What each verdict actually means

1 · Published standard

Age and category determine the maximum qualifying time. This is the threshold to apply, not an acceptance guarantee.

2 · Competitive cutoff

The 2027 cutoff is a verified outcome, not a forecast. To clear it, the adjusted time must be at least 5:17 faster than the published standard.

3 · Random selection

The B.A.A. introduced a separate random qualifier-selection opportunity for qualifying applicants who missed the competitive cutoff. Being eligible for consideration is not selection.

Practical questions

Can I still enter using a 2027 qualifying time?

The 2027 qualifier registration window closed September 18, 2026 and will not reopen. Other entry pathways have separate eligibility and availability; do not assume a qualifying result creates an open registration slot.

Does the random qualifier selection guarantee a bib?

No. It was a limited random opportunity for eligible applicants in the closed registration process. The checker can flag possible consideration, never selection.

Does a net downhill course invalidate my result?

Not automatically. Official adjustments begin at 1,500 feet of net downhill. At 6,000 feet or more, the course is ineligible under the cited rules. Course certification and official verification remain essential.

What about 2028?

The 2028 qualifying window began September 19, 2026. Do not apply the 2027 acceptance cutoff to 2028; its cutoff and registration decisions are not determined by this page.

When do waves start and how should spectators travel?

Exact 2027 wave times and spectator transport schedules are not asserted here. Check B.A.A. race-week guidance and local transit advisories when officially published. The Boston Marathon is a point-to-point course; a spectator should not infer travel times from distance alone.

Evidence, edge cases and how to interpret this calculator

Not an official registration decision. The official race organizer verifies submitted results and decides acceptance. This tool only performs arithmetic against published open-field standards. A runner who clears the published cutoff but did not submit an eligible application during the closed September 2026 window is not thereby registered. A submitted application with an unverified or ineligible course also cannot be treated as an accepted entry.

Race-day age matters. The qualifying category uses your age on April 19, 2027. For example, someone who ran a qualifying marathon at age 34 but turns 35 before Boston race day uses the 35–39 standard, not the 18–34 standard. The checker asks for the race-day age explicitly; it does not infer your age from a birthdate or store personal information.

Downhill adjustments are not a speed bonus. A net drop of 1,499 feet does not trigger the published adjustment, while 1,500 feet adds five minutes to the recorded time for this screening. At 3,000 feet the adjustment rises to ten minutes; at 6,000 feet the course is not eligible under these rules. Elevation is a start-to-finish net measurement, not total descent or cumulative downhill along a hilly route. Verify official course data rather than estimating from a fitness watch.

Cutoff comparison is second-accurate. A 35-year-old man with a 3:00:00 standard must have an adjusted time of 2:54:43 or faster to clear a 5:17 competitive cutoff. A 2:54:44 result misses the competitive threshold by one second, although it remains faster than the qualifying standard. This is precisely why the tool displays both thresholds independently.

Random-selection possibility is narrower than qualification. The separate random qualifier opportunity applies to eligible applicants in the relevant closed process who missed the competitive cutoff. The checkbox is a user assertion, not an application lookup. It does not show whether a runner was selected, and it does not reopen registration or confer a bib.

Geographic and division limits. A qualifying result from outside Massachusetts can still require organizer verification of course certification and race results. This open-field model does not evaluate wheelchair, para-adaptive, invitational, charity, sponsor or other entry pathways. These have separate published conditions and must not be inferred from a general qualifying-time result.

Future-year counterexample. A race run after the 2027 qualifier window may belong to the next qualifying cycle, but this checker deliberately does not extrapolate a 2028 competitive cutoff. A fast 2028-eligible performance cannot be screened for 2028 acceptance using the confirmed 2027 cutoff.

Privacy and reproducibility. All inputs are processed locally in your browser; no personal result is submitted to EventV. The calculation uses the time you enter and the published standards. Retain your official race certificate, course details and organizer correspondence for any actual acceptance dispute.
