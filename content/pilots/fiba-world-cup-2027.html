How to use this without making an expensive mistake

Begin with a dated official fixture and venue, then enter its Doha-local start. Add your own expected match duration, exit time and transfer buffer. A green conflict check only means your entered assumptions do not overlap. It cannot prove transport feasibility, venue access or ticket validity.

Counterexample: two games may start three hours apart, yet post-match exit, road closures, security screening and queueing can make the second one unreachable even if an organizer describes venues as within roughly 30 minutes of one another.

Practical questions

Are these real match fixtures?

No. Every fixture row is entered by the visitor. No unverified bracket, team qualification, venue or kickoff is supplied.

Will my local match time account for daylight saving?

Yes, the browser converts a Doha UTC+3 instant through the selected IANA timezone. A viewer in London or New York may see a different date and seasonal offset.

Does a pass comparison guarantee entry?

No. The descriptions summarize FIBA's advertised pass categories. Eligibility, inventory, assigned games, terms, seat allocation and checkout must be checked directly with FIBA.

Can I rely on a 30-minute transfer?

No. It is an organizer-level proximity description, not a door-to-door guarantee. Use a conservative custom buffer and verify accessible routes, transit and security requirements.

Do I need a visa or can I stream every game?

Entry rules depend on nationality, documents and current Qatar requirements; broadcasting rights depend on territory. This page does not assert universal entry or streaming access.

Where is my data stored?

Scenarios are kept in your own browser's localStorage, when available. Export creates a local JSON file; clearing removes this tool's local key. Shared devices require care.
