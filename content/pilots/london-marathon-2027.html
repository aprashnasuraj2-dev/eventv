Two dates, different finish boundaries

Saturday 24 April

The organiser says the finish line on The Mall closes at 17:30. It gives a maximum Saturday completion time of 7 hours 45 minutes in its FAQ.

Sunday 25 April

The organiser says the secondary finish in Green Park closes at 23:59:59. This is a different location and boundary from Saturday’s Mall finish.

Your assigned day

The organiser says assigned in-person days cannot generally be changed and swapping a place can lead to cancellation. Check your own official entry.

These are dated organiser statements, not an individual start time, entry entitlement or guarantee of the same finish facilities throughout each day. Verify current instructions for your place.

Estimate a constant-pace scenario

Enter your own hypothetical start time and pace in decimal minutes per kilometre. For example, 6.5 means 6 minutes 30 seconds per kilometre. The calculation uses 42.195 km at a constant speed and reports an estimated duration and clock time; it does not apply a cutoff or establish your wave.

A constant pace ignores congestion, breaks, terrain, fatigue and actual timing rules. The result is not a prediction or advice about a safe effort. Check your official allocated wave and event instructions.

What the 2027 double means

The organiser describes a one-off two-day format on 24 and 25 April 2027, with the same course on both days. It lists the start at Greenwich/Blackheath. A participant’s allocated event day matters for travel, collection and the appropriate finish rule; the weekend dates are not interchangeable tickets.

The organiser’s FAQ says the Saturday Mall finish closes at 17:30. It states that the most time available to complete the Saturday marathon is 7 hours 45 minutes and instructs anyone who believes they need longer not to pay for a Saturday entry and to contact its customer engagement team. A calculator cannot decide an individual’s start time or whether they will finish before the line closes.

On Sunday the organiser describes a secondary finish line in Green Park, closing at 23:59:59. Its overview says that participants falling behind eight-hour pace on Sunday may receive manual bib scans as some timing equipment is packed away. Do not infer that the main Mall finish or all services operate until the secondary line closes; check the latest detailed participant guide.

The FAQ calls possible Saturday and Sunday start times likely, while the overview says participants will be emailed their wave approximately three weeks before their marathon day. Those statements are not the same as a confirmed individual wave. Use the actual communication from the organiser when it arrives, and recheck event updates.

Entry: distinguish possibility from a place

The organiser says the 2027 public ballot was open from 24 April to 1 May 2026 and will not reopen. Its entry overview lists other pathways, such as charity, Good For Age, club, championship and international entry. A listed pathway does not establish that applications remain open, that you qualify or that an organisation has an available place.

Check the current rules, age and residency criteria, supporting evidence, fees, deadlines and allocation with the organiser or the specific charity or international partner. A charity application can have separate fundraising commitments. An application, an offered place, payment and a confirmed entry are different stages.

The official FAQ says participants cannot generally swap event days with another person, and doing so can invalidate a place. It also says the allocated day cannot generally be changed. If your circumstances create a problem, contact the organiser through its official channels rather than buying or transferring a place informally.

The organiser describes a separate MyWay option and separate registration for each MyWay day. A remote participation product is not an in-person marathon place; verify current terms and what the product includes before applying. This guide does not assert that either product is currently available.

Understand the pace arithmetic

At an assumed constant six minutes per kilometre, 42.195 km takes approximately 4 hours 13 minutes 10 seconds, rounded to the nearest second. If a participant chooses a hypothetical 09:00 start, the mathematical finish is 13:13:10. That is an illustration, not an official wave, likely finish or guarantee. The tool computes duration using seconds before rounding, so a fractional minute pace can produce a fractional second.

Because the actual course experience is not perfectly constant, a group using the estimate should consider their own potential delays and check the organiser’s timing guidance. Small differences in speed accumulate over 42.195 km. The model does not account for breaks or disruptions, and an estimate earlier than a closure does not guarantee that the participant can access a particular finish or will receive a recorded result.

The form uses London local clock time and explicitly labels results that cross midnight. It does not convert a visitor’s home time zone or infer which day a person has been assigned. Keep the date, participant and official event instructions alongside any scenario.

Spectator and travel checklist

  1. Confirm the participant’s actual day, start wave, support needs and an agreed meeting plan. Avoid publishing personal tracking details without consent.
  2. Use the current official course and transport advice to choose a reachable location. Road closures and pedestrian crossings can make two nearby map points difficult to connect on the day.
  3. Check local transport operators for service changes and step-free access at the stations you intend to use. The finish area and participant reunification arrangements can differ by time and day.
  4. For international travel, consult current official UK entry requirements and carrier rules for the traveller’s circumstances.
  5. Check the organiser’s latest participant guide for bib collection, permitted items, accessibility, medical and cut-off arrangements. The scenario tool does not provide live tracking.
