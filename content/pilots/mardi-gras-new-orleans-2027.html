Understand the dates and route evidence

Mardi Gras Day is 9 February 2027 according to the city. New Orleans & Company describes Carnival beginning on 6 January, with parades in the intervening weeks. A trip on Fat Tuesday is one possible plan; it should not be confused with the entire season or with a confirmed schedule for every krewe.

The tourism parade page lists 2027 events while also saying that final parade dates and routes are coming soon. Treat that as a reason to verify each individual event close to travel, especially when booking a non-refundable product. The city’s event-map service warns that parade times and dates can change because of weather or public safety.

Use the city’s current route and transport information to select a viewing location. A parade listed in a neighbourhood does not establish its precise route, accessible crossings, transit diversion or when the front or end of the parade reaches your position. The city’s official parade tracker and current instructions are more appropriate than a static prediction.

Public right-of-way and setup rules

NOLA Ready states that personal items should not be placed on a public right-of-way earlier than four hours before a parade and must not block intersections. The same page also warns that items placed four hours or more before a parade may be removed. Because that language treats the exact boundary cautiously, do not assume that setting something out at exactly four hours is guaranteed compliant. Check the current city code and follow staff directions.

The city’s guidance also describes limits on ladders, including height and distance from the curb, and says not to fasten them together or leave them overnight. It lists open flames, fuel generators and structures enclosing public space among restrictions. The city’s full guidance is more detailed than this guide and can change; consult it before bringing equipment.

Do not block a pedestrian path, intersection, emergency access or a space needed by other people. Rules for private property and public rights of way can differ, and a private viewing reservation does not grant control of a public sidewalk. Confirm requirements with the city or venue for your situation.

Travel, crowds and accessibility

The same-day comparison tool subtracts your entered travel allowance from the time between two activities. If one ends at 14:00, another starts at 15:00 and you allow 40 minutes to move, it reports a 20-minute margin. That is not a prediction: a crossing may be closed, a parade may run late or transit may be diverted. The form rejects a second time earlier than the first rather than silently assuming the next day.

Plan a meeting point and a return route that do not depend on crossing a closed parade route. Check current transit operator notices and accessible boarding arrangements. For specific wheelchair, sensory, companion or medical needs, ask the event organiser or relevant venue for current accessible routes and facilities. A route map alone is not a promise of an unobstructed view.

NOLA Ready advises checking prohibited items and provides safety resources. Use current city guidance for first-aid locations, lost children, weather and emergency information. In an emergency in the United States, call 911. For international travel, check applicable entry requirements with responsible US authorities.

  1. Confirm the specific event’s current date, time and route with its organiser and city notices.
  2. Check transport diversions, parking restrictions and permitted pedestrian crossings.
  3. Read current city guidance on personal items and prohibited equipment.
  4. Budget actual transport, accommodation, food and any private viewing products; do not treat the calculator as a quote.
