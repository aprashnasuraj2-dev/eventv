Compare a trip with your own bloom scenario

Enter your travel dates and an entirely user-supplied possible bloom window. The form counts inclusive overlap with both that hypothetical window and the published festival dates. It does not predict flowering or imply the two overlaps occur on the same days.

The assumed window is your input, not an NPS forecast. The tool counts calendar days, not hours, flower condition, hotel availability or event tickets.

Understand “peak bloom”

The National Park Service uses a specific observation: peak bloom is the day when 70% of the Yoshino cherry blossoms are open. It is not the first blossom, a guarantee that every tree looks the same, or the entire period when a visitor might see flowers. Different tree varieties can bloom at different times.

Blossom development depends on weather. Historical dates or an average do not determine the 2027 peak. A forecast published closer to the event may change if conditions change. The appropriate source for the current Tidal Basin forecast and observed stages is the National Park Service’s Bloom Watch, not a fixed date calculated from a previous year.

The festival organiser’s 20 March–11 April dates describe a cultural programme. They can be set long before weather determines when blossoms open. A visitor can attend festival events without peak bloom, and a peak can occur outside the festival period. Both statements can be true at once.

How the date comparison works

Suppose a trip runs from 25 to 29 March and a visitor enters a hypothetical bloom window of 27 to 31 March. The inclusive overlap is three calendar days: 27, 28 and 29 March. All five trip dates fall within the published festival range. Neither number says the trees will actually be at peak or that a particular activity takes place on those days.

The form checks valid calendar dates, rejects reversed ranges and counts both endpoints. A trip ending on the first day of an assumed window has one overlapping calendar day. It does not estimate what time a person arrives or leaves; an arrival late in the evening still counts as a date, so the number is not equivalent to full sightseeing days.

The hypothetical bloom overlap and festival overlap are calculated independently. If a trip spans both periods, the two counts do not automatically identify the same calendar dates. Check the actual date ranges yourself when making reservations. No assumed window is saved or sent to the organiser.

Choose events and protect the trees

The festival’s site lists a range of events across the region. A festival-wide banner appears on multiple event pages; look for the specific event’s own date, venue, accessibility, admission and weather information. A ticket or registration for one activity does not automatically cover another.

For the Tidal Basin and National Mall, check the National Park Service’s current visitor guidance and transit information. Crowds, path works and weather can affect access. Stay on designated paths where directed and follow park guidance to protect trees and shared spaces. Ask the relevant event or park staff about accessible routes and facilities for your needs.
