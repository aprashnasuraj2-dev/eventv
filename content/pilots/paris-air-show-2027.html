Compare a display and a meeting

When an official programme is published, enter a hypothetical display interval and a meeting interval on the same local day. The tool calculates overlapping minutes. It does not know where you will be, whether an aircraft flies or whether you have access.

Touching endpoints produce zero overlap, but moving between places still takes time. Weather, safety decisions and programme changes can alter flying displays.

Trade days, public weekend and students

The organiser describes trade days for professional visitors followed by a weekend open to the general public. Its general-public page also says students are invited to a free Friday with online registration. These are different access pathways. A professional badge, a student registration and a public ticket may involve different rules, and this guide does not determine which applies to an individual.

Before booking, verify the exact day, access category, proof required, registration process and availability on the official website. The published show range does not imply that every day is open to every visitor or that student registration is still available. If travelling as a group, verify conditions for each person.

The organiser’s pages include statistics and highlights from the 2025 show. Those numbers describe a past edition, not confirmed 2027 exhibitors, aircraft, visitors or attendance. The 2027 programme was marked as coming soon at the source check; consult the current official programme when it is published.

How to read an overlap

If an assumed display runs 14:00–14:30 and a meeting runs 14:20–15:00, the tool reports ten minutes of overlap. If one ends exactly when the other begins, it reports zero overlap. That does not allow for the time needed to move from the display area to the meeting room, find an entrance or queue.

The form compares time intervals only and requires both to start and finish on the same day. It does not identify an actual aircraft, predict whether a demonstration will fly, or account for weather and operational decisions. Use the organiser’s current programme and instructions, and choose a practical transfer allowance separately.

For a hypothetical group of two, €35 per ticket and €40 in shared costs total €110, or €55 each. These figures are invented for illustration. They do not represent a Paris Air Show price, a student entitlement or a parking quote.

Getting to Le Bourget

The organiser warns that private-vehicle access may be restricted because of attendance and external constraints. Check current official directions and transport-operator services before choosing rail, bus, shuttle, taxi or parking. A route that worked for a previous edition may be altered.

For access needs, ask the organiser about accessible transport, viewing areas, seating and companion arrangements for the specific day. Check permitted items and security rules. Outdoor conditions, crowds and noise can affect comfort; bring what is permitted and appropriate to your circumstances.

  1. Confirm the current ticket or registration category for your chosen day.
  2. Check the official programme and any flying-display updates before arranging meetings.
  3. Verify current transport, parking restrictions, venue entry and accessibility details.
  4. For international visitors, consult the relevant French and Schengen authorities about entry requirements.
