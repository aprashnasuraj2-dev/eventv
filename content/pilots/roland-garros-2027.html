The outside-court boundary changes

The cited 2027 Roland-Garros Travel terms use three date ranges. From 23 to 29 May, they describe courts 2–14 and the upper stand of Simonne-Mathieu as outside courts. On 30 and 31 May, all of Simonne-Mathieu joins courts 2–14 in that definition. From 1 to 6 June, Suzanne-Lenglen also joins Simonne-Mathieu and courts 2–14 in the definition.

These labels are not the same as a reserved place on every court. The terms discuss access according to the specific ticket and session, and outside-court access can be affected by capacity or changed conditions. A night-session ticket holder’s outside-court access is subject to the applicable stadium entry time in the terms. Check the actual ticket and current official guidance before relying on any description.

The lookup is restricted to 23 May–6 June because that is the period covered by the quoted definition. An opening-week ticket may have different terms. For example, a query for 29 May gives the upper stand of Simonne-Mathieu; the next day gives all of that court under these terms. That is a change in the stated court category, not a guarantee of an available seat.

Individual tickets, hospitality and travel packages

The official September 2026 notice warned that individual 2027 tickets had not yet gone on sale and identified separate official ticketing, hospitality and travel channels. Because that was a dated notice, it cannot establish current inventory or today’s sales date. Check the official tournament and ticket portal before paying, especially when a site claims to have an individual ticket before an announced sale.

A travel package can include tickets together with hotel or other services; individual ticketing and hospitality can have different conditions. Check the actual provider, what is included, date, court, day or night session, delivery method, refund and transfer rules. An advertised package is not evidence that the draw is complete or that a specific player will appear.

The official first-round travel page lists 23–25 May and notes that the daily match schedule is known later. The official finals package identifies women’s and men’s finals on 5 and 6 June respectively. Match assignments, participants, times and operational details can change; the named dates do not identify the finalists.

Compare session products with care

The organiser’s travel terms distinguish day and night sessions on Philippe-Chatrier during the dates when its schedule is divided. The official first-round offer describes the day and night products separately. Do not assume a day ticket automatically admits you to the night session or vice versa; read the specific dated product’s terms and entry window.

For a hypothetical two-person trip, a €400 package per person and €100 additional shared costs add up to €900, or €450 each when shared equally. These are invented figures, not a current package price. A cheaper-looking ticket can have additional accommodation, tax, travel or transfer costs. Conversely, adding a hotel that is already part of a package double-counts the cost.

Before travelling to Paris

  1. Check the tournament’s official ticket source and your own ticket’s date, court, session, entry conditions, transfer and refund rules.
  2. Verify current local transport, service changes and accessible station routes with the relevant operator. Check stadium entry directions and allowed items with the organiser.
  3. Contact the organiser for accessible seating, companion arrangements or other individual access needs; this guide cannot verify availability.
  4. For international visitors, check applicable French and Schengen entry requirements with the responsible official authorities and confirm carrier terms.
  5. Recheck the official order of play after it is published and allow for weather, match duration and schedule changes when planning a return journey.
