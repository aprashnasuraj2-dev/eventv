A new city and different viewing windows

Boulder, not Utah

Sundance lists 21–31 January in Boulder, Colorado. The festival moves to Boulder beginning in 2027; its 2026 edition was in Utah.

Public online window

The organiser describes public at-home screenings from 28–31 January for specified selections, with some other films able to opt in and limited audiences.

Professional access

The organiser describes press and industry online access beginning 27 January. This is a different audience and access path from the public window.

The announced online window does not mean every film is available online, in every country or without capacity restrictions. Verify the exact film and ticket terms.

Times and distances are not preloaded. Weather, queues, accessibility needs, a long Q&A or a late screening can change the real connection.

Enter the number of intended screenings, a hypothetical single-ticket price and a hypothetical pass or package cost. This arithmetic assumes the pass covers all selected screenings, which must be verified from the actual product; it is not a ticket quote.

A pass may have selection windows, screening limits, blackout conditions or fees. A lower hypothetical amount does not establish availability or guaranteed seats.

What changes in Boulder

Sundance announced Boulder as the festival’s home beginning in 2027. The 21–31 January 2027 dates therefore belong to Colorado, while the preceding 2026 edition took place in Utah. Do not use a Park City venue map, shuttle arrangement or hotel booking from an older edition as a Boulder itinerary.

The institute lists venues on the University of Colorado Boulder campus, including Macky Auditorium, Roe Green Theatre and Muenzinger Auditorium, as well as Boulder Theater, Chautauqua Auditorium and venues at the Dairy Arts Center. Its announcement describes Cinemark Century as a press and industry screening venue. A listed venue is not proof that a particular film, public screening or seat is available there.

The organiser says the festival footprint is within roughly a two-mile radius. A radius is not an actual walking route, travel time or accessibility guarantee. Winter weather, hills, road conditions and lines can affect movement between venues. Check the official venue map, current transport and individual accessibility information.

In person, online and professional access

Sundance’s planning announcement describes world premieres from 21–26 January and additional showings throughout the festival. It also describes public at-home screenings from 28–31 January for films in its five competition sections, including NEXT, with films in other sections able to opt into a limited-audience online programme. Verify the actual film listing and territorial, capacity and viewing-window conditions when available.

Press and industry online access is described as starting one day earlier, 27 January. This does not give every visitor a right to watch on that date. Professional credentials, public tickets, in-person passes and online access can have different eligibility and selection procedures. The institute’s industry page describes a separate registration process.

An organiser announcement can state a planned sales period without proving a particular product is available today. Check the current official ticket site for pass selection, single-film inventory, fees, refund terms and any online restrictions. This guide does not reproduce prices from a previous festival edition.

Build a workable screening day

If a screening ends at 14:00, the next starts at 15:30, and you allow 20 minutes for an additional Q&A plus 40 minutes for travel and entry, the hypothetical margin is 30 minutes. If the first programme already includes the Q&A in its published end time, adding the full 20 minutes again would double-count it.

For a separate price illustration, five hypothetical $25 single tickets total $125. An imagined $100 pass would appear $25 cheaper only if it actually covers those five screenings and has no additional fees. Neither amount is a Sundance quote. A pass that cannot reserve a sold-out screening may not provide the same practical access as a confirmed single ticket.

  1. Check the current official programme, venue, start and end time for each screening.
  2. Confirm the ticket or credential, selection process and whether Q&A time is included in the displayed schedule.
  3. Use current venue accessibility and transport information and allow extra time for queues and winter conditions.
  4. For international travel, verify current US entry requirements with the responsible authorities.
  5. For online viewing, check the exact film’s availability, region, dates and device conditions.
