What UEFA has announced

Final date and venue

UEFA lists Saturday 29 May 2027 at the National Stadium in Warsaw. The women’s final is a separate event from the men’s final in Madrid.

Published clock

UEFA writes 18:00 CET. Warsaw normally uses summer time in late May, so confirm whether the organiser means local Warsaw time or literal fixed CET before converting it.

Ticket channel

UEFA says final tickets will be sold exclusively through UEFA.com/tickets and invites fans to register for updates. This does not establish current inventory or price.

Finalists, broadcaster details and actual ticket availability are not established here. The return planner requires your own assumed kickoff and does not resolve the time-zone wording.

Why the CET label needs checking

UEFA’s final page calls the published time 18:00 CET. Under current time-zone data Warsaw observes Central European Summer Time in late May. If the label CET is meant as the local regional clock, 18:00 Warsaw local and literal 18:00 fixed CET refer to instants one hour apart. This guide does not assume which meaning UEFA intends. Check a later UEFA match page and local stadium or broadcaster information before booking time-sensitive transport.

A user-entered kickoff in the return tool is explicitly hypothetical. It is not loaded from the ambiguous UEFA label, and the model compares only a departure later on the same calendar day. For an after-midnight service, use a separate dated itinerary instead of treating 00:30 as earlier than an evening match.

From league phase to Warsaw

UEFA describes an 18-team league phase in 2026/27. The top four in the overall standings advance directly to the quarter-finals; teams placed fifth through twelfth enter two-legged knockout play-offs; positions 13 through 18 are eliminated. The four play-off winners then join the top four in the quarter-finals.

Quarter-finals and semi-finals are two-legged rounds before the final at the neutral venue. A club appearing in the league-phase list is not automatically a finalist. UEFA’s final guide states that the 2026/27 winner receives a place in the following season’s league phase. Check the official standings, draw and match results as the competition progresses.

The competition is the UEFA Women’s Champions League. UEFA’s men’s Champions League final has a different venue and date; a ticket or broadcast listing for one event should not be assumed valid for the other.

What happens if the final is level?

UEFA says that if the score is level after normal time, two 15-minute extra-time periods are played. If the score remains level after extra time, a penalty shoot-out determines the winner. The return tool’s 90-minute baseline models normal play only; the user must add half-time, stoppage and other interruptions, and use a separate allowance for any extra time, additional stoppage, breaks and shootout.

For example, with an assumed 18:00 kickoff, 25 minutes of breaks and stoppage, 45 minutes for exit and travel, and a 21:00 departure, the normal-play scenario leaves 20 minutes. Adding a user-chosen 50-minute extended-match allowance makes the scenario 30 minutes short. These numbers are invented for arithmetic and do not predict a match duration or service.

A match can overrun, security and crowds can delay departure, and a transport service can be cancelled. Even if the calculation produces a positive margin, it does not guarantee arrival before doors close or that a ticket is valid. Consider a later option and verify the current transport schedule.
