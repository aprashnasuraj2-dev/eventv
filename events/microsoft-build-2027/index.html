Why this page is different

Build announcements mix product maturity levels that have very different engineering consequences.

One Build keynote can contain demos, private previews, public previews, generally available services, pricing changes, deprecations, new SDKs and features that roll out later. Those states are not interchangeable. A production team needs to know what exists now, who can use it, what it costs, what minimum versions it requires, what changes are breaking, and what deadline applies.

AnnouncementA capability can be real without being available to your tenant, region or subscription yet.
PreviewPreview terms, support boundaries, APIs, quotas and pricing can differ from GA.
Generally availableGA still does not guarantee that your existing SDK, region, SKU or architecture is compatible.
Migration requiredA small release note can be more urgent than a keynote headline when it carries an enforcement or retirement date.

Counterexample: a heavily promoted new AI capability may have zero immediate impact on your production stack, while a quiet SDK minimum-version change can block your next deployment. Keynote prominence ≠ engineering priority.

Engineering workflow

A practical Build verification sequence

  1. Freeze your pre-event baseline.Record production SDKs, API versions, Azure SKUs, regions, runtime versions, CLI tooling and critical dependencies.
  2. Capture the primary Microsoft source.Prefer Learn documentation, Azure updates, release notes, official product blogs and Build session pages over recap articles.
  3. Separate maturity.Announcement, private preview, public preview, GA, rollout and deprecated/retiring must remain distinct states.
  4. Check commercial scope.Record pricing meter, quota, subscription requirements, tenant eligibility and region availability separately from feature availability.
  5. Check compatibility.Record minimum SDK/API/runtime, supported regions and migration guidance before changing production.
  6. Test rollback.For breaking or cost-sensitive changes, prove a rollback/canary path before deadline pressure arrives.
